SMP-Video from the SMP Challenge (smpchallenge/SMP-Video on Hugging Face, gated): short TikTok-style videos posted between Jan 2022 and Aug 2023. This page describes the whole labelled train file, 4,000 posts. The dataset’s test files hold 2,000 more posts with no popularity label, so they are not used. The label popularity is a continuous score (mean 8.26, SD 2.86). Its 90th percentile is 11.69 (400 posts at or above it). No train/test split is defined yet.

2  Clean dataset

2,167posts (US and English), each with a video description
1,922distinct users
5user groups by account size
25posts discarded (description blocked)

Built by smp/pipeline/ from the 4,000 labelled posts. The split is in section 3.

Steps

  1. Filter: keep posts with post_location = US and post_text_language = en (2,192 of 4,000).
  2. Group by account: predict popularity from the account alone, then cut that prediction into five equal groups, so posts are compared with posts from similar accounts. 
    expected_popularity = 3.84 + 1.64 × log10(followers)
                    − 1.26 × log10(videos posted)
                    − 0.10 × log10(following)
    Likes, heart and digg counts are left out: they partly include the post’s own likes.
  3. Drop the account columns: the 7 user_* counts are removed, so only the group carries account information; post_location and post_text_language too, being the same on every row.
  4. Describe every video: Gemini (gemini-3.5-flash) watches each video and writes a full text record: summary and fields, a timeline, the timed transcript and on-screen text. It sees only the video. 25 descriptions were blocked by Gemini’s recitation filter (mostly long, music-heavy videos); those posts are discarded, leaving 2,167.

Why this grouping

Seven ways to group the 2,192 posts by account. “Variance removed” is how much popularity differs between groups; “follower ρ inside groups” is the account effect left over within a group (lower is better).

E removes nearly as much as the best options while leaving almost no follower effect and keeping groups large. F leaves too few posts per group once a test set is split off, and G uses likes per video, which partly includes the post’s own likes.

Model input

Every model reads one text block per post, model_text (median about 700 tokens):

Caption: Mission Give everyone glass skin clearskin skintips
Posted: Sunday, 2022-10-09 14:45 UTC
Music: original sound
Video length: 7 seconds
Orientation: vertical
Resolution: 540p
Video:
  Summary / Content type / Topic / Format / Hook / People / Setting /
  Filming / Editing / Audio / Tone / Call to action / Products or brands
  Timeline: one line per scene, with its audio
  Transcript: timed spoken lines
  On-screen text: timed texts

Never shown to a model: ids, popularity, the label, and the account group. Left out on purpose: post_suggested_words (search suggestions added by the platform; they track popularity within every account group, so possibly a leak).

3  Split

1,731 / 436train / test posts
353 / 84train / test YES
0users on both sides
Top 20%within each user group = YES

Built by smp/pipeline/prepare_data.py into smp/data/split/train.parquet and test.parquet.

  1. Split by user, 80/20: all of a user’s posts go to one side, stratified by follower quintile.
  2. Refit the groups on train only (intercept 3.73, followers +1.62, videos -1.24, following -0.04); test posts get the same model and edges.
  3. Label: YES = popularity in the top 20% of the post’s own group, with the cutoff taken from that group’s training posts. The groups only set the cutoff; one pooled model is trained and tested on all groups.

Why pooled: five separate groups would give test sets of about 86 posts and 17 positives each (AUC uncertain by about ±0.15). Pooled, the test set has 436 posts and 84 positives (about ±0.07), and the label still controls for account size. Results per group are possible but rough.

Content predicts the within-group label above chance: TF-IDF reaches 0.68, and among the 20% of posts it ranks highest, 40% are truly in their group’s top 20% (twice the base rate). RRF (0.618) and PolicyInduction (0.595) are below it; PolicyInduction’s gap is outside the noise, RRF’s is borderline. Adding PolicyInduction’s policy scores to TF-IDF does not change the result.

RRF’s questions are concrete and checkable (a gift’s story, audio used as commentary, synchronised handling sounds, exact quantities); PolicyInduction’s policies are broad quality judgements. Weights come from regressions over overlapping features, so individual signs should not be read as findings.
